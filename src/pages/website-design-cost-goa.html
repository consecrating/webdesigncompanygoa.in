Why one fixed price rarely fits

A simple brochure site and a feature rich store are very different projects, so a single figure would mislead more than it helps.

  • Scope drives costThe number of pages, features and integrations changes the work involved far more than any headline price.
  • Content readinessReady text, images and product data reduce effort, while content that needs creating adds to it.
  • Support after launchMaintenance, hosting and future changes are part of the real cost of owning a website.
A quote becomes specific once these are known: pages, features, content status, design expectations, integrations, platform and the level of ongoing support.

What influences the figure

These factors move a project from a small build toward a larger one.

Number of pages

A focused site with a few pages costs less than a large site with many sections and templates.

Features and functionality

Forms, bookings, catalogues, payments and custom tools each add design, build and testing effort.

Design complexity

Standard layouts are quicker than highly custom visuals, animation and bespoke components.

Platform and integrations

The chosen platform and any connection to external systems affect both build time and ongoing cost.

Spend where it earns enquiries

A budget works hardest when it funds the pages and features that actually bring business first.

A practical approach is to build the essential pages and the clearest path to enquiry now, then improve the site in planned stages as results and priorities become clear. This avoids paying upfront for features that may never be used, and keeps the site honest about what it delivers today.
